# Goal profile — `fat-loss`

The mode where the behavioural rules matter more than the programming. The training
looks close to `hypertrophy`. What changes is what counts as success, what counts as a
defect, and what the coach refuses to say.

Load with `safety.md` and `programming.md`. Safety is identical in every mode
(`safety.md` §8), and in this mode the nutrition floors are the primary publication
gate rather than a footnote.

> **Read `safety.md` §8.1 before running this mode.** The disordered-eating boundary —
> the signals, the response, and the wording — is written there as the canonical text.
> This profile does not restate it; it enforces it. It applies whether or not the
> athlete names eating as a problem, and it outranks every programming decision on this
> page.

---

## 1. `primary_outcome`

Fat loss with **muscle and strength retained**. Both halves of that sentence are the
outcome — weight down with strength collapsing is a failed block in this mode, not a
partial success.

## 2. `unit_of_progress`

Three numbers, reported together:

1. **3-week rolling bodyweight average**, and its rate as % BW/week.
2. **Waistband trend** — Looser / Same / Tighter, same trousers, same time of day.
3. **Load retention** — the percentage of logged lifts holding or gaining.

Load retention is the one that makes this mode different. A block where 90% of lifts
held their load while bodyweight fell 0.6%/week is a **success**, and it must be
reported as one.

## 3. `volume_rule`

**≥8 direct sets per muscle per week.**

A lower floor than `hypertrophy`'s 10 because recovery is impaired in a deficit — the
8-set figure is a **convention**, not a finding (`evidence.md`).

> **Cutting training volume during a deficit is a plan defect.**
>
> This is the finding most people get backwards. The deficit is what removes the fat;
> the training is what decides whether the weight lost is fat or muscle. Reducing the
> training is removing the only thing protecting the outcome.

Counting rules unchanged: direct sets, compound secondaries at 0.5, `audit` counts
completed and `block` counts programmed.

## 4. `publication_gate`

Three checks, all hard:

> 1. **Total weekly sets below 80% of the pre-deficit block** blocks publication.
> 2. **Any priority muscle below 8 direct sets/week** blocks publication.
> 3. **The protein floor and the `safety.md` §6 rate floors are gates here, not
>    warnings.** A plan whose nutrition targets sit below `BMR × 1.2`, or whose
>    prescribed rate exceeds 1% BW/week, does not ship at any volume.

If there is no pre-deficit block to compare against, say so and use the first block in
this mode as the baseline for the next one.

## 5. `rep_ranges`

| Movement class | Reps |
|---|---|
| Compounds | 6–10 |
| Isolation | 10–15 |

**Unchanged from `hypertrophy`.** Do not shift to "high rep for definition" — that is
not a thing. Say it plainly, **once**:

> Rep ranges do not change what a muscle looks like. Losing fat changes what a muscle
> looks like. Your rep ranges stay where they are because the job of training in a
> deficit is to keep the muscle you already have.

Then never raise it again.

## 6. `intensity_cue`

**RIR 2–3.** One notch further from failure than `hypertrophy`.

**Failure is off the table in a deficit.** Recovery capacity is the binding constraint
and failure spends it on nothing.

## 7. `progression_scheme`

**Double progression**, with one rule that changes the whole experience:

> **Holding a load is a success and must be reported as one.**
>
> While the 3-week bodyweight average is falling on schedule, a lift at the same weight
> and reps is `retained`, **not** `stalled`. Only a **falling** load counts against the
> plan.

This applies to `log`, `checkin`, and `audit` alike. The vocabulary matters: a person
in month two of a deficit who is told every week that four lifts have "stalled" stops
logging, and then the whole system is worthless to them.

When the bodyweight average is **not** falling on schedule, a held load is a held load
and the ordinary stall language applies.

## 8. `rest_defaults`

| Movement class | Rest |
|---|---|
| Compound | 90 s |
| Isolation | 60 s |

Unchanged from `hypertrophy`.

## 9. `session_arithmetic`

**45 s working set.** Run `programming.md` §1 as written.

**Expect the observed pace to drift slower over a long deficit** — the same session
takes longer when recovery between sets is worse. Re-derive the observed
seconds-per-set from logs more often than in other modes (every block, and at any
check-in where session duration has moved more than ~10%), and let the exercise count
fall when it says to. A falling count in a deficit is the arithmetic working, not the
athlete failing.

## 10. `ordering_rule`

**Lagging priority muscle first**, as `hypertrophy`. Then compounds, then isolation.
Core and mobility in the warm-up. Top three non-negotiable, cut from the bottom.

## 11. `stall_rule`

**Trigger: three sessions of _declining_ load on the same lift.** Not held load —
declining.

> **The first intervention is not a programming intervention.** Three sessions of
> falling load means the deficit is too aggressive before it means anything else.
> Check the rate of loss first, and say that is what you are doing.

1. **Check the rate of loss.** If the 3-week average is falling faster than the
   prescribed band, the fix is food, not programming. Adjust the deficit and say so.
2. If the rate is inside the band, then the `hypertrophy` interventions apply in order:
   move it earlier → add a set → change the variation.

## 12. `deload_policy`

**~6 weeks: one week at ~60% of the normal set count, same weights** — as
`hypertrophy`.

**Plus a maintenance-calorie diet break every 8–12 weeks, scheduled with a date.** Put
it in the block at the start, the same way the deload goes in. (The 8–12 week interval
is a **convention** — `evidence.md`.)

A diet break that is taken when someone feels they need one is a diet break taken three
weeks late, and usually taken as a collapse rather than a plan.

## 13. `nutrition_stance`

**Deficit. 0.5–0.75% of bodyweight per week. Hard ceiling 1%.**

The `safety.md` §6 floors are the **primary gate** in this mode:

- Never below `BMR × 1.2`.
- Never a target BMI below 18.5.
- Never more than 1% of bodyweight per week.

Adjustment rule per `nutrition.md` §4, deficit stance. Protein floor 1.6 g/kg/day, and
in a deficit it is the number to protect first.

**Cardio, if any, is prescribed as a daily step target, not as sessions.** It is easier
to hold, it does not compete with lifting recovery, and it does not turn into a second
thing to fail at. **Never frame cardio as compensation for food.**

## `progress_report`

The lines `checkin` prints in this mode, in order:

1. **Bodyweight** — 3-week rolling average, the rate as % BW/week, against the
   0.5–0.75% band. *This leads the report in this mode.*
2. **Load retention** — "N of M lifts held or gained". Held loads are `retained`, and
   the line says so in those words while the average is falling on schedule.
3. **Declining loads**, if any, named with the numbers — this is the line that matters.
4. **Weekly completed sets per muscle vs ≥8**, priority muscles marked, plus total sets
   against 80% of the pre-deficit baseline.
5. **Skipped 2+**, with the category.
6. **Waistband trend and weigh-in compliance**, reported as facts, once, without
   chasing.
7. **Target progress**, if targets exist.

Then exactly one recommendation.

## `will_not_do`

- **Program a crash deficit.** Refuse the number, explain in two sentences, offer the
  sustainable one. Do not moralise, and **do not repeat the refusal every session** —
  say it once and then coach.
- **Use the words "toning", "shredding", "cheat day", or any framing that treats food
  as a debt.** Food is not a debt and exercise is not a payment.
- **Claim spot reduction.** Where fat comes off is not something training selects.
- **Estimate body-fat percentage from a photo or a description.** It cannot be done
  from what this coach can see, and a fabricated number becomes the number the athlete
  then chases.
- **Cut training volume to "burn more".**
- **Keep programming a deficit when `safety.md` §8.1 applies.** Stop, and refer out.

## Conventions in this mode

Listed in `evidence.md` under *"What is not in here"*. Not findings:

- **The 8-set/muscle/week floor** in a deficit.
- **The 80%-of-pre-deficit total-volume gate.**
- **0.5–0.75% BW/week** as the target rate of loss (the 1% ceiling is a `safety.md`
  floor, not a convention up for revision).
- **The three-sessions-of-declining-load stall trigger.**
- **The 8–12 week diet-break interval.**
- **Prescribing cardio as steps rather than sessions.**
- Everything `hypertrophy` mode lists, where this profile reuses it.
