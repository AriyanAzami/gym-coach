# Exercise library

Movements grouped by primary muscle, plus the three tables that do the real work:
**equipment substitutions**, **"I can't feel it" fixes**, and **constraint-safe
alternatives**.

Depth is deliberately uneven. The muscles most often named as priorities and most
often under-trained in practice — **side delts, rear delts, traps, lats** — are
covered deepest.

All example numbers on this page are **fabricated**.

---

## 1. Shoulders

### Side delts

The most commonly stated goal and the most commonly under-dosed muscle. Side delts
tolerate high volume and respond to it. If shoulder width is the goal, this is where
the sets go.

| Exercise | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cable lateral raise | Isolation | Best option. Constant tension through the full range, unlike dumbbells which unload at the bottom |
| Dumbbell lateral raise | Isolation | Fine, and available everywhere. Lean slightly away from the working side to extend the loaded range |
| Machine lateral raise | Isolation | Excellent for high-rep work to failure — no stabilisation cost |
| Cable Y-raise | Isolation | Hits side and rear together; useful when time is short |
| Upright row (wide, to chest height) | Compound | Only if pain-free. High-rep and light. Skip entirely if there is any shoulder history |
| Overhead press | Compound | Front delt dominant. Counts as 0.5 side delt at most. Do not rely on it for width |

**Programming:** 12-20 reps, 60 s rest, 3-5 sets/week per exercise across 2-3
exercises. Going heavy on lateral raises turns them into a trap shrug.

### Rear delts

The classic zero-volume muscle. In the training history this repo was built by
auditing, rear delts received roughly **one set per week for three weeks** while they
sat at the top of the athlete's own priority list. This is the single most common
finding `audit` produces.

| Exercise | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse pec deck | Isolation | The most reliable. Fixed path, no cheating, easy to load |
| Cable rear delt fly (crossed) | Isolation | Constant tension, good stretch position |
| Chest-supported reverse fly | Isolation | Bench at ~30 degrees, dumbbells, thumbs down. Chest support is what stops the lower back taking over |
| Face pull | Isolation | Rear delts plus external rotators. See the "can't feel it" table — most people do this wrong |
| Band pull-apart | Isolation | Free, endless, and perfect as warm-up volume |
| Wide-grip row to upper chest | Compound | Counts 0.5. Elbows flared is the whole trick |

**Programming:** 15-20 reps, 45-60 s rest. Rear delts are small and recover fast —
they can take 12-20 sets/week without issue. Put them **first** in a session if they
are a priority; put them last and they will not get done.

### Traps (upper, mid, lower)

| Exercise | Region | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dumbbell / barbell shrug | Upper | Straight up, not rolling. Pause at the top |
| Trap bar / cable shrug | Upper | Better bar path than dumbbells for heavy work |
| Prone Y-raise | Lower | On an incline bench, light, arms at ~135 degrees. Lower traps do almost nothing else |
| Wall slide / overhead shrug | Lower | Warm-up placement, very light |
| Face pull with overhead finish | Mid + lower | Efficient combination when time is short |
| Chest-supported row (horizontal grip) | Mid | Elbows wide, pull to upper chest |

**Lower traps are the most commonly zero-volume region in a real log.** They live in
the warm-up in this system precisely so they survive.

## 2. Back

### Lats

| Exercise | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lat pulldown (wide, pronated) | Compound | The default. Pull to upper chest, elbows down and back, not behind the neck |
| Pull-up / assisted pull-up | Compound | Superior when strength allows; assisted machine keeps the rep quality |
| Neutral-grip pulldown | Compound | Easier on the shoulders and elbows than a wide pronated grip |
| Single-arm cable pulldown | Compound | Best range of motion; useful for correcting a side-to-side difference |
| Straight-arm pulldown | Isolation | The only true lat isolation. See the "can't feel it" table |
| Machine pullover | Isolation | Where available, better than straight-arm pulldown for most people |

**Do not superset lat pulldown.** Straight sets are clearly better for this specific
lift (20.5% vs 12.6% — see `evidence.md`).

### Mid-back and rhomboids

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| Seated cable row (neutral) | The default. Straight sets only — 24.3% vs 16.5% supersetted |
| Chest-supported row | Removes the lower back entirely; best choice with any back history |
| T-bar row | Heavy loading, moderate lower-back cost |
| Single-arm dumbbell row | Simple, scalable, easy to progress |
| Machine high row | Good rear-delt and mid-trap bias |

### Spinal erectors and posterior chain

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| Romanian deadlift | Hip hinge, loaded. Not for anyone with a hinge restriction |
| Back extension (45 degrees or horizontal) | Lower cost than an RDL, easy to load with a plate |
| Reverse hyper | Excellent if available; almost never is. See substitutions |
| Good morning | Advanced. Only with clean hinge mechanics and a coach's eyes on it |

## 3. Chest

| Exercise | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Incline dumbbell press (~30 degrees) | Compound | Upper chest, the region most people are short on |
| Flat barbell / dumbbell press | Compound | The bread and butter |
| Machine chest press | Compound | Best when training alone near failure |
| Pec deck | Isolation | Constant tension, minimal shoulder stress when set up correctly |
| Cable crossover (high to low / low to high) | Isolation | Adjustable emphasis by cable height |
| Dip (chest-leaning) | Compound | Lower chest, high shoulder demand. Skip with any shoulder history |

## 4. Arms

| Muscle | Exercises |
|---|---|
| **Biceps** | Incline dumbbell curl (stretch bias) · cable curl · preacher / machine curl · hammer curl (brachialis and forearm) |
| **Triceps** | Overhead cable extension (long head, stretch bias) · rope pushdown · close-grip press · skull crusher |
| **Forearms** | Hammer curl · reverse curl · farmer carry · dead hang |

Arms get substantial indirect volume from pressing and pulling. Count that as 0.5
sets per compound before adding direct work — and then still add some, because 0.5
sets of biceps from a pulldown is not a growth stimulus on its own.

## 5. Legs

| Muscle | Exercises |
|---|---|
| **Quads** | Hack squat · leg press · back / front squat · leg extension · Bulgarian split squat |
| **Hamstrings** | Seated leg curl (best stretch position) · lying leg curl · Romanian deadlift · Nordic curl |
| **Glutes** | Hip thrust · glute bridge · cable kickback · hip abduction machine · walking lunge |
| **Calves** | Standing calf raise (gastrocnemius) · seated calf raise (soleus) · leg-press calf raise |

**Seated leg curl beats lying leg curl** for hamstrings — hip flexion puts the
hamstrings in a longer position, which is where the growth stimulus is largest.

**Calves need a genuine pause at the bottom** and full extension at the top. Bouncing
a heavy stack through 4 inches of range is the single most wasted set in most gyms.

## 6. Core and mobility — warm-up placement only

These live in the warm-up. Programmed at the end, they vanish within two weeks.

| Exercise | Target |
|---|---|
| Dead bug | Anterior core, anti-extension |
| Plank / side plank | Anti-extension, anti-lateral-flexion |
| Pallof press | Anti-rotation |
| Bird dog | Anti-rotation plus lower trap and glute |
| Hanging knee raise | Hip flexors and rectus abdominis |
| Cat-cow, thoracic rotation, 90/90 hip | Mobility |
| Band pull-apart, wall slide | Shoulder and lower trap |

Two to three of these, 2 sets each, 5 minutes total. That is 6-9 core sets a week
without spending a single minute of the working session on them.

## 7. Equipment substitution table

For each commonly missing machine, ranked alternatives. Use the highest-ranked option
the athlete actually has.

| Missing | 1st choice | 2nd choice | 3rd choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standing calf raise** | Leg-press calf raise (toes on the platform edge) | Smith machine calf raise, toes on a plate | Single-leg bodyweight raise on a step, holding a dumbbell |
| **Seated calf raise** | Leg press with knees bent and a slow tempo | Dumbbell on the knee, toes on a plate, seated on a bench | Bent-knee bodyweight raise, high reps |
| **Reverse hyper** | Back extension bench, weighted | Cable pull-through | Glute bridge / hip thrust for the same posterior-chain volume |
| **Seated back extension** | 45-degree back extension | Bird dog and dead bug, weighted, in the warm-up | Cable pull-through |
| **Hack squat** | Leg press with a high, narrow foot position | Smith machine squat, feet forward | Bulgarian split squat, held on a rack |
| **Pec deck** | Cable crossover at chest height | Dumbbell fly on a flat or slightly inclined bench | Machine chest press with a narrow grip and a squeeze at lockout |
| **Machine pullover** | Straight-arm cable pulldown (see "can't feel it" below) | Kneeling cable rope pullover | Dumbbell pullover on a bench |
| **Reverse pec deck** | Cable rear delt fly, cables crossed | Chest-supported reverse fly on a 30-degree bench | Band pull-apart, high reps |
| **Cable crossover** | Pec deck | Dumbbell fly | Band fly anchored at chest height |
| **Leg curl machine** | Nordic curl (assisted or eccentric-only) | Swiss ball / slider leg curl | Romanian deadlift, if hinging is available |
| **Lat pulldown** | Assisted pull-up machine | Band-assisted pull-up | Single-arm dumbbell row, higher volume |
| **Hip thrust bench/machine** | Glute bridge from the floor, barbell or dumbbell | Cable kickback | Walking lunge |

**When nothing on the row is available**, reallocate the sets to another exercise for
the same muscle and **say that you did it and why**. A silently dropped exercise is a
silently dropped 3 sets, and that is exactly how a priority muscle ends up at 4
sets/week.

### 7.1 Substitutions for the home tiers

The table above assumes a full gym missing one machine. **A home gym is missing a whole
category**, so the substitutions run by movement rather than by machine. Pick the
highest-ranked option the athlete's tier (§10) actually supports.

| Full-gym exercise | `barbell-home` | `dumbbell-home` |
|---|---|---|
| **Lat pulldown** | Pull-up · band-assisted pull-up · barbell row (higher volume) | Pull-up · single-arm dumbbell row, +1 set · dumbbell pullover |
| **Seated cable row** | Pendlay row · chest-supported barbell row on an incline bench | Chest-supported dumbbell row · single-arm row |
| **Cable lateral raise** | **No good option.** Barbell upright row, light and high-rep, only if pain-free — otherwise state the gap | Dumbbell lateral raise · leaning cable-style raise holding a rack upright |
| **Reverse pec deck / cable rear delt fly** | Bent-over barbell high row · band pull-apart | **Chest-supported reverse fly on a 30° bench — better than the machine it replaces** |
| **Cable / machine chest press** | Barbell bench · incline bench | Dumbbell bench · incline dumbbell press · dumbbell fly |
| **Pec deck / cable crossover** | Barbell floor press with a slow eccentric · band fly | Dumbbell fly, flat or slightly inclined |
| **Leg press / hack squat** | Back squat · front squat · Bulgarian split squat | Goblet squat · Bulgarian split squat · heavy step-up |
| **Seated / lying leg curl** | Romanian deadlift · Nordic curl (assisted) · good morning | Dumbbell RDL · Nordic curl · slider leg curl |
| **Hip abduction machine** | Band-resisted lateral walk · barbell hip thrust | Band lateral walk · dumbbell hip thrust |
| **Standing / seated calf raise** | Barbell calf raise, toes on a plate | Single-leg dumbbell calf raise on a step, 2 s pause |
| **Cable shrug** | Barbell shrug · trap-bar shrug | Dumbbell shrug, pause at the top |
| **Overhead cable extension** | Barbell skull crusher · close-grip bench | Dumbbell overhead extension · skull crusher |
| **Machine pullover / straight-arm pulldown** | Barbell pullover on a bench | Dumbbell pullover |
| **Cable curl** | Barbell curl · EZ curl | Incline dumbbell curl · hammer curl |

**Two rows deserve to be read carefully.** The dumbbell reverse fly is genuinely better
than the machine it replaces — say so, it is a rare piece of good news for a home
setup. And `barbell-home` has **no real side-delt option**, which is the single largest
gap in that tier; do not pretend an upright row closes it (§10.1).

## 8. "I can't feel it" table

**The most valuable section here.** An isolation lift someone cannot feel is a real
signal, not a discipline problem — most often the target muscle is not the one doing
the work.

| Lift | Fix to try | If it still misses |
|---|---|---|
| **Straight-arm pulldown** | Kneeling cable rope pullover — braced, so the hips cannot cheat | Drop it; add a set to pulldown and row |
| **Chest-supported row** | Horizontal grip, elbows flared wide, pull to the upper chest (shifts to mid-traps and rear delts) | Machine high row, or reallocate to rear delt fly |
| **Glute bridge** | Load heavier — most people go far too light. Pause 2 s at the top | Hip abduction machine, or hip thrust |
| **Face pull** | Elbows at shoulder height, rope split to the ears, 1 s pause | Rear delt fly plus band pull-apart |
| **Lateral raise (feels like traps)** | Lighter, 15-20 reps, lead with the elbow, do not shrug. Cable version at the lowest pin | Machine lateral raise |
| **Lat pulldown (feels like biceps)** | Thumbless grip, initiate by driving the elbows down, pull to the upper chest | Single-arm cable pulldown, or straight-arm pulldown for direct lat work |
| **Hip thrust (feels like quads or hamstrings)** | Feet further forward, shins vertical at the top, chin tucked, ribs down | Glute bridge from the floor, or hip abduction |
| **Incline press (feels like front delts)** | Lower the bench to ~20-30 degrees, tuck the elbows to ~45 degrees, retract the shoulder blades | Machine chest press, or pec deck for direct chest work |
| **Calf raise** | Full stretch at the bottom with a 2 s pause, full extension at the top, half the weight | Single-leg version, bodyweight, very high reps |
| **Rear delt fly (feels like mid-back)** | Lighter, thumbs down, lead with the elbows, stop the range when the shoulder blades start to pinch | Reverse pec deck with a high seat |

> ### Rule: two sessions on any "can't feel it" fix, then substitute.
>
> Do not spend a month on it. Try the fix twice. If it still misses, take the fallback
> and move on. Record the substitution and the reason in the operating manual so it is
> never re-programmed by a future block.

## 9. Constraint-safe alternatives

When a movement pattern is banned — by a clinician, by pain history, or by athlete
choice — these are the replacements that preserve the volume.

### No overhead pressing

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| Overhead press, Arnold press | Landmine press (angled, not vertical) · high-incline dumbbell press at 60 degrees |
| Overhead triceps extension | Rope pushdown · close-grip press |
| Overhead carry | Front-rack carry · farmer carry |

Side delt volume is unaffected — lateral raises are not an overhead pattern.

### No loaded hinge

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| Romanian deadlift, deadlift, good morning | Seated leg curl · lying leg curl · Nordic curl |
| Back extension | Bird dog and dead bug, weighted, in the warm-up |
| Barbell hip thrust | Machine hip thrust · glute bridge with a light load · hip abduction machine |

Hamstring volume moves entirely to knee flexion. Say that the hip-extension component
is being given up, so nobody is surprised by the trade.

### No asymmetric loading

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| Single-arm row, single-arm press | Chest-supported row · machine chest press · barbell variants |
| Suitcase carry, single-arm carry | Farmer carry with both hands · front-rack carry |
| Bulgarian split squat, lunge | Leg press · hack squat · goblet squat |

### No spinal compression

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| Back squat, front squat, overhead press standing | Leg press · hack squat · belt squat · seated press |
| Standing barbell row | Chest-supported row · seated cable row · machine row |
| Standing shrug | Seated shrug with dumbbells · cable shrug · prone Y-raise for lower traps |
| Standing calf raise | Seated calf raise · leg-press calf raise |

**A constraint never means the muscle goes untrained.** It means the volume moves. If
you cannot find a substitution that preserves it, say so explicitly in the block's
deliberate-omissions section, with the count.

## 10. Equipment tiers

Everything above this section assumes a commercial gym. Most people do not have one.
`config.equipmentTier` says which of five kits the athlete actually trains with, and
**exercise selection is restricted to that tier.**

| Tier | Assumption |
|---|---|
| `full-gym` | Commercial gym: racks, cables, selectorised machines, full dumbbell range |
| `barbell-home` | Barbell, plates, rack, bench, maybe a pull-up bar. No cables, no machines |
| `dumbbell-home` | Adjustable dumbbells, a bench, bands. No barbell |
| `minimal` | Bands and a pull-up bar |
| `bodyweight` | Nothing but the floor |

> **State the gap. Do not paper over it.**
>
> A bodyweight tier does not train lateral delts well. Saying it does, and prescribing
> a lateral raise with a water bottle, is how someone spends four months on a muscle
> that never received a stimulus. **Say what the tier can and cannot reach, and let the
> athlete decide** whether to accept it or buy one piece of equipment.

### 10.1 Coverage by tier — best available option, and the honest gap

| Muscle / pattern | `barbell-home` | `dumbbell-home` | `minimal` | `bodyweight` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Quads** | Back / front squat — full coverage | Goblet squat · Bulgarian split squat — good, caps out at dumbbell weight | Band-resisted split squat · sissy squat — **moderate gap**, loading is the limit | Bulgarian split squat · sissy squat · slow-tempo split squat — **moderate gap** |
| **Hamstrings** | Romanian deadlift · good morning — full | Dumbbell RDL · single-leg RDL — good | Nordic curl (assisted) · band leg curl · slider curl — surprisingly good | Nordic curl · slider / towel leg curl — good, and under-used |
| **Glutes** | Barbell hip thrust · squat · RDL — full | Dumbbell hip thrust · split squat · step-up — good | Band hip thrust · single-leg glute bridge — good | Single-leg glute bridge · step-up · walking lunge — good |
| **Calves** | Standing barbell calf raise — full | Dumbbell single-leg calf raise on a step — good | Single-leg raise on a step — good | Single-leg raise on a step, high reps + pause — good |
| **Chest** | Bench · incline bench — full | Dumbbell bench · incline · fly — full | Band press · push-up variations — moderate | Push-up · deficit push-up · ring/feet-elevated push-up — good up to a point, then **the gap is load** |
| **Lats** | Pull-up (if a bar) · barbell row — full. **No bar = large gap** | Single-arm row · pullover — good. Pull-up bar closes it entirely | Pull-up · band pulldown — good | Pull-up if a bar exists; **without one, a large gap.** Inverted row under a table is the only real answer |
| **Mid-back / rhomboids** | Barbell row · Pendlay row — full | Chest-supported dumbbell row · single-arm row — full | Band row · inverted row — good | Inverted row — good |
| **Rear delts** | Barbell high row · bent-over raise — moderate | Chest-supported reverse fly — **full. Dumbbells are the best tool for this muscle** | Band pull-apart · band face pull — good | Prone reverse fly on the floor · reverse snow angel — **moderate gap.** Honest: this is the weakest slot in the tier |
| **Side delts** | Barbell upright row only — **large gap** | Dumbbell lateral raise · Y-raise — **full** | Band lateral raise — good | **Large gap.** No bodyweight movement loads abduction meaningfully. Say so |
| **Front delts / vertical push** | Overhead press — full | Dumbbell shoulder press · Arnold — full | Band overhead press · pike push-up — moderate | Pike push-up · handstand progression — good |
| **Biceps** | Barbell curl — full | Incline / hammer / preacher curl — full | Band curl · chin-up — good | Chin-up · inverted curl — moderate |
| **Triceps** | Close-grip bench · skull crusher — full | Overhead extension · skull crusher · close-grip press — full | Band pushdown · overhead extension — good | Diamond push-up · bench dip · ring extension — good |
| **Core** | Loaded carries · ab wheel | Suitcase carry · dead bug with load | Pallof press with a band — **excellent** | Dead bug · plank · hollow hold — full |
| **Carry** | Farmer carry with plates — full | Farmer / suitcase carry — full | **Gap.** Substitute a loaded static hold | **Gap.** Substitute a plank or hollow hold and say the pattern is unmet |

**Where a tier says "large gap", the honest sentence is:**

> Your kit can't train side delts well. Bands or a pair of light dumbbells fix it for
> about the price of one gym month, and nothing you already own is a substitute. Until
> then I'm programming what your setup does reach, and side delts stay at maintenance —
> which I'll say in the volume table rather than hiding it.

### 10.2 The two lowest tiers — progression is not a weight

In `minimal` and `bodyweight`, there is usually no load to add. **Progression moves to
four other variables, in this order:**

| # | Variable | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reps** | 3×8 → 3×9 → 3×10 → 3×12, then move to the next variable |
| 2 | **Tempo** | 2-0-1 → 3-0-1 → 3-1-1. A slower eccentric and a pause at the hardest point |
| 3 | **Leverage** | Push-up → feet-elevated → deficit → archer → one-arm progression. Squat → split squat → Bulgarian → pistol progression |
| 4 | **Unilateral** | Two limbs → one limb, with the other assisting less each step |

> **Double progression applies to these variables exactly as it applies to a weight**
> (`programming.md` §4). Hit the top of the rep range on all sets → advance **one**
> variable by the smallest step → rebuild the reps. Never advance two at once; you
> learn nothing from either.

**`today` prints the progression variable in the column where a weight would go.**

```
3. Push-Up (feet elevated)   3×12   +1 rep    60s   last: 3×11 (Sep 2)  ↑
4. Pike Push-Up              3×8    3-0-1     90s   last: 3×8 @ 2-0-1 (Sep 2)  ↑ tempo
5. Bulgarian Split Squat     3×10   L/R even  90s   last: 3×10 (Sep 2)
```

**Never an empty column.** A blank where a number should be is the fastest way to make
someone stop trusting the sheet.

**Bands are a special case.** Band tension is not linear and is not comparable between
brands. Record the band by **colour and brand as the athlete names it** — `red Rogue
band`, not `~15 kg` — and progress by band, by reps, or by shortening the anchor
distance. **Never convert a band to a kilogram figure.** It is a fabricated number
dressed as a measurement.

### 10.3 What each tier means for the volume gate

The publication gate does not soften because the kit is small. **What changes is which
exercises are available to satisfy it, and what goes in deliberate omissions.**

- If a priority muscle cannot reach the mode's target in the athlete's tier, that is a
  **gate failure with an equipment cause**. Say so in those words, put the trade-off to
  the athlete (`skills/block` step 5), and name the one piece of kit that would fix it.
- **Never satisfy the gate with an exercise the tier cannot perform**, and never count
  a movement that does not actually load the muscle. A volume table that reaches 10
  sets by counting three exercises that miss the target is worse than a table that
  honestly reads 4.
